Preparation and Differences Between Acetylene, Benzene, and Formaldehyde

This laboratory experiment investigated the reactivity of acetylene gas (C₂H₂), benzene (C₆H₆), and formaldehyde (HCHO) with potassium permanganate (KMnO₄), Baeyer's reagent, and ammoniacal silver nitrate (Ag[(NH3)2]NO3) to identify the presence of specific functional groups. Acetylene gas, prepared via a water displacement method using calcium carbide, exhibited expected reactivity with both reagents. When reacted with Baeyer's reagent, a noticeable color change from purple to brown occurred, indicating the formation of manganese dioxide (MnO₂) as a brown precipitate. This reaction confirms the presence of a carbon-carbon triple bond in acetylene. With ammoniacal silver nitrate, a white precipitate, silver acetylide (Ag₂C₂), was formed, further confirming the presence of acetylene.Benzene (C₆H₆) did not react with Baeyer's reagent (KMnO₄), consistent with its high stability and resistance to oxidation. However, a white precipitate formed when benzene was reacted with ammoniacal silver nitrate (Ag[(NH3)2]NO₃). This unexpected result suggests the presence of impurities or contaminants in the reagents or glassware, which may have reacted with the silver nitrate. Formaldehyde (HCHO) reacted with Baeyer's reagent (KMnO₄), producing a brown precipitate, indicating the presence of a carbonyl group. However, no significant reaction was observed with ammoniacal silver nitrate (Ag[(NH3)2]NO₃).

Qualitative Analysis of Anions Using Spot Plates ( Experiment)

In the positive control test for carbonates, when 1 mL of 1M sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃) was mixed with 1 mL of 6M hydrochloric acid (HCl) in a test tube, effervescence was observed immediately. This bubbling indicated the release of carbon dioxide gas (CO₂), confirming the presence of carbonate ions (CO₃²⁻) in the solution.

In the negative control test, water was used in place for sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃). After it was mixed with 1mL of 6M HCL, no effervescence was observed, even after the test tube was placed in a hot water bath to encourage any possible reaction. This lack of bubbling confirmed the absence of carbonate ions.

Test for the presence of phosphate ion, PO₄²⁻

In the positive control test for the phosphate anion, the formation of a yellow precipitate was clearly observed. A noticeable amount of the precipitate adhered to the stirring rod during mixing, confirming the presence of phosphate ions.

In the negative control, distilled water was used in place of the sodium phosphate solution. No visible reaction or color change occurred, and no precipitate was formed throughout the procedure. This confirms that the test does not produce a false positive in the absence of phosphate ions.

Test for the presence of thiocyanate ion, SCN⁻

For the positive test of thiocyanate ions, we first added 0.5 M KSCN solution, which indicates the expected presence of SCN⁻. This was followed by the addition of 1 mL of 6 M acetic acid and then 2 drops of 0.1 M Fe(NO₃)₃. A dark red solution immediately formed, indicating a positive control test for the presence of thiocyanate ions. This is the expected outcome because the solution was known to contain SCN⁻ ions.

For the negative test of thiocyanate ions, 1 mL of distilled water was used instead of the KSCN solution. The same procedure was followed by adding 1 mL of 6 M acetic acid and then 2 drops of 0.1 M Fe(NO₃)₃. A light orange color was observed, but no dark red solution indicating the presence of thiocyanate ions was seen. This confirms the absence of thiocyanate ions in the sample.

Test for the presence of nitrate ion, NO₃⁻

For the positive test for Nitrates Ions, KNO₃ is added to the test tube that represents the sample, 6M of NaOH and a few granules of Aluminum metal are added to the sample and boiled in a hot water bath, to detect the presence of Nitrates, litmus paper is used. Litmus paper is placed on top of the test tube (but not in contact with), and red litmus paper turns blue confirming the presence of Nitrates due to the vapor produced by the solution.

For the negative test, distilled water is used instead of KNO3 and the same reagents are added. Same process to the positive test have been done to negative test but without adding KNO3. You can also observe rising of gas (vapor) but no change of color in red litmus paper placed near at the mouth of test tube. This confirms that nitrate ions were not present.
